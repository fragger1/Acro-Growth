Two segments, one outbound engine

A starting plan for Luath Medical: Apollo cold email plus LinkedIn organic and paid
Prepared by Ahmed M. (AcroGrowth) · October 2026 · This draft is based only on your job post. I'd refine it with your messaging, case studies and target geographies at kickoff.

1. How the engine is shared and where it splits

Shared across both segments
One Apollo workspace and sending setup (secondary domains, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warm-up). One LinkedIn presence. One weekly report, split by segment. One set of Luath proof points: case studies, process, team.
Separate for each segment
The list, the buying trigger, the offer, the copy, the cadence and the call to action. Startup founders and OEM engineering managers buy for different reasons, so they never get the same sequence.

2. Targeting, signals and messaging by segment

Segment 1: Early-stage startupsSegment 2: OEMs & established suppliers
Who Founders, CEOs, CTOs and R&D leads at pre-seed to Series A device companies (roughly 2–30 staff). Engineering Managers, Directors of Engineering, R&D Managers and Product Development leads at device OEMs, contract manufacturers and component suppliers.
Why now Time pressure: a raise or grant just landed, or a bench test, clinical build or funding round is coming up. Capacity pressure: the team is stretched by a launch, a line transfer or open roles that haven't been filled.
Qualifying signals
  • Funding round or grant in the last 12 months
  • No or very few mechanical/design engineers on LinkedIn
  • Pre-clearance: no 510(k)/CE mark yet; an early feasibility study planned
  • Founder posting about prototypes, first-in-human or fundraising
  • Mechanical design, R&D or manufacturing engineer roles open for a long time or reposted
  • Recent 510(k) clearance or product launch (sustaining and launch load)
  • New site, expansion or line-transfer announcements
  • 50+ staff, with an internal engineering team already in place
Data sources Crunchbase/Dealroom and Apollo funding filters; Enterprise Ireland, EIC Accelerator and SBIR/STTR award lists; MedTech Innovator and BioInnovate Ireland cohorts; ClinicalTrials.gov device studies; LinkedIn headcount by title. Apollo/LinkedIn industry filters and NAICS 339112, 339113, 334510; FDA 510(k) database; job boards; Irish Medtech Association members; exhibitor lists from MD&M West, MEDICA/COMPAMED and Medical Technology Ireland.
Exclude Software-only (SaMD) and digital health; pharma/biotech; startups that already have an engineering team in place. Distributors and sales-only offices; software-only companies.
Offer "An investor-ready prototype, then bench or clinical builds, without a full-time hire." "A specialist alongside your team for defined work: DFM, tolerance stack-ups, design reviews, tooling and assembly. No new headcount."
Tone Founder to founder. Short and direct, about their timeline and their next raise. Engineer to engineer. Precise and technical, with no hype words. Reads like a scope, not a pitch.
Call to action A 15-minute call about their timeline. Low commitment: "Want a one-page overview?" Then a call when the next crunch comes.

3. First-touch emails (drafts)

Subject: {{company}} prototype

Hi {{first_name}},

Congrats on {{signal, e.g. closing the pre-seed / the EIC award}}.

Most device founders at your stage need a working prototype and bench data before the next round, but a full mechanical team doesn't make sense yet.

Luath fills that gap. We take a concept to an investor-ready prototype and on into bench testing or clinical builds, working as your engineering team until you're ready to hire one. [one-line Luath startup case study]

Worth 15 minutes to see if your timeline fits?

{{sender}}

Sequence: 4 touches over about 2 weeks: signal + offer → case study → a useful insight (what investors want from a prototype) → close the loop. Plus a LinkedIn connect.

4. LinkedIn: organic and paid

Organic

  • Post from a person (founder or lead engineer). Personal posts usually travel further than company pages.
  • 2–3 posts a week, alternating audiences: DFM and stack-up lessons for OEMs; concept-to-prototype stories for founders.
  • Connect with prospects after email 1 and engage with their posts, so the name is familiar before follow-ups land.

Paid

  • Matched Audiences: upload both account lists, so ads only reach companies already being emailed.
  • Thought Leader Ads to boost the best organic posts; Document Ads with a gated checklist (DFM for OEMs, prototype-readiness for founders).
  • Small budget first. Scale only what produces replies and meetings, not just clicks.

5. First 30 days

Week 1: Set up

Kickoff on messaging and ICP limits. Audit Apollo sending (domains, authentication, warm-up). Build and verify ~250 accounts per segment. Draft all copy for your sign-off.

Week 2: Launch

Both sequences go live at low volume. LinkedIn profile tune-up, first posts, connection outreach. Nothing goes out without your approval.

Week 3: Learn

Read every reply. A/B test the angle per segment (signal-led vs proof-led). First paid test: Matched Audience plus a Thought Leader Ad.

Week 4: Adjust

Monthly review: what's working by segment, what gets cut, and the plan for month 2.

6. What you'd see every week

  • By segment: contacts reached, replies, positive replies, meetings booked and held
  • By list source and angle: which signal and message produced conversations
  • Health: bounce and spam-complaint rates; LinkedIn acceptance, ad CTR and cost per lead
  • Next week: the changes I'm making and why
