## Cover letter (paste from "1." down)

> **1. Why a 20-person Flemish contractor ignores the email**
>
> Trust. They've never heard of you, and nothing in a typical vendor email shows you understand how their business runs, so replying looks like all risk: a sales call, a demo, and a tool the crew won't use. (If it's in English, that alone is enough.)
>
> What I'd change: make it read like it came from a person who knows their week, not from a software company. Write it in Flemish, from one of you by name, and say plainly that you're a small Belgian company. Open with one moment the owner or estimator actually lives through, like pricing a tender at night or chasing extra work that never got invoiced, whichever problem your tool solves. End with one question they can answer yes or no from their phone. No demo request, no feature list. And stop asking email to do the selling: its job is to make the phone call that follows feel expected.
>
> **2. The channel I'd lead with: the phone**
>
> Owners of 5–50 person firms run the business from their mobile and tend to pick up, because a call might be a new job. Estimators can be reached at the office. A two-minute call in Flemish gets you a yes, a no or an objection the same day, and objections are what you need most at this stage. An ignored email tells you nothing. I'd back each call with a short letter or email a few days earlier, so the call has a reason, and test call times around site hours (early morning, late afternoon). What makes me confident is that it's quick to prove wrong. After the first 100–150 dials we'll know the connect rate, how many calls become real conversations, and the top objections. If the phone loses to letters or email, we switch in week 2, not month 3.
>
> **Who makes the calls**
>
> To be upfront: the calls need to be in Flemish, and I don't speak Dutch. For the first campaign I'd suggest one of you takes the calls in two or three fixed blocks a week. Founders hearing objections first-hand is worth more than any report I could write. I'd own everything around those blocks, so every hour on the phone goes to the right firms with the right script.
>
> **First campaign: $1,000 fixed, about 4 weeks**
>
> - Week 1: rank the first 150–200 firms on your list. I can add each firm's website and Google Maps details to decide who to call first. Write the Flemish call script, the objection answers and the pre-call letter or email, with you checking tone. Set up a simple tracker.
> - Weeks 2–3: the call blocks run. After each block we do a short debrief; I adjust the script and call order and send the follow-ups.
> - Week 4: a report on what actually happened: dials, connects, conversations, interested firms, reply and positive reply rates on letters and emails, every objection and how often we heard it, and what I'd change for campaign 2.
>
> Not included: postage and printing for letters, and phone costs. (Upwork made me enter an hourly rate because the job is posted as hourly; the fixed price above is my number.)
>
> **Where I'm coming from**
>
> My outbound work is with trades and small manufacturers. For a US client, I built the system behind their outreach to auto repair shops, machine shops, metal fabricators, commercial printers and equipment dealers. I won't pitch you a lead database or a deliverability stack: you have the lists, and email is the wrong lead channel for this audience anyway.
